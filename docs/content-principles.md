# Content Principles

Immigration content is high-impact and often time-sensitive. Pathport should be useful without pretending to be an official authority.

## Source Hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Official government immigration pages.
2. Embassy or consulate pages.
3. Official legislation or policy pages.
4. Recognized international organizations.
5. Reputable legal, relocation, or tax firms.
6. Community reports and personal experience.
7. AI-assisted summaries.

Lower-trust sources can be useful, but they should not override official sources.

## Required Metadata

Every content piece should eventually track:

- source URLs / source-document records
- citations to the specific field or block the source supports
- last reviewed date
- confidence level
- destination country
- relevant citizenship, route, route applicability, or audience/assumption scope
- notes about uncertainty, conflicts, or missing data

This applies beyond routes: country facts, living-cost rows, entry rules,
language reads, family/pet rules, route details, and applicability facts all need
sourceability. S6 should replace route-only sources with general source documents
and content citations.

## Tone

Use plain language. Avoid legalistic confidence unless quoting or summarizing official requirements.

Prefer:

- "may be eligible"
- "usually requires"
- "official source says"
- "verify before applying"

Avoid:

- "you qualify"
- "guaranteed"
- "always"
- "legal advice"

## Data Quality Labels

Pathport can use visible quality labels when appropriate:

- `Official source`
- `Needs review`
- `Estimate`
- `Community note`
- `Outdated`
- `AI-assisted draft`

These labels should help users understand what they are looking at without making the UI feel alarming.

## AI Use

AI can help draft summaries, identify route categories, compare requirements, and normalize messy research notes.

AI-generated content should be treated as a draft until checked against sources. The product should be explicit when content has not been verified.

## Legal Boundary

Pathport should not present itself as legal advice. It should help users discover and compare options, then point them toward official sources and qualified professionals where needed.
